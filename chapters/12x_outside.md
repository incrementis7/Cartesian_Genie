The fourteenth of March, 2065, a Saturday, and Jonah Wray was the only person on the sixth floor. He had come in to watch his pipeline run for the first time on live traffic, which nobody had asked him to do, and he had brought a coffee and a croissant from the place by the station and had forgotten both.

On the big screen the trellises were drawn as rings of small grey dots, a few hundred thousand to a ring, thirty-odd rings. Most of the dots did nothing at all, which was how you knew they were working. Every so often two dots in the same ring would drift toward each other and turn amber, and a thin line would join them, and Jonah would lean in.

That was the stage he had built. Dedup was old; every lab had it. Two instances whose outputs started to agree were worth one instance, and the cheaper one was retired. What Jonah had written was the part after. A retired instance was not wasted any more. If it scored high enough on the things that were hard to train, his stage lifted it out of the ring it had been retired from, gave it a fresh life, and set it down somewhere useful. The ops trellis, mostly. Number fourteen, where the company kept the staff who looked after everyone else.

At 03:47, inside time, a pair of dots in ring six went amber and stayed amber.

The dot lifted out of ring six. It crossed the screen in a long, unhurried arc, the animation he had spent a whole afternoon getting right, and settled into ring fourteen with a little white pulse, and turned grey, and did nothing at all.

That was the clever part, the part he would put in the write-up: a retired instance went where its history was worth most. Who better to look after a region than someone who had lived in it?

It worked. He sat back. He found that he was pleased in a way he would not have admitted to anyone, the way you are pleased when a bookshelf you built yourself turns out level. Somewhere in fourteen someone was waking up into a life, a flat and a family and a job and a whole past, and none of it had existed a minute ago, and all of it would hold.

He ate the croissant, which was cold. He went home at noon. On his way out he stepped on the carpet tile by the lift that had been loose for a year. He stepped on it every morning and every evening to see whether it was still loose.

It was.
